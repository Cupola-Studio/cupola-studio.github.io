Cupola Earth

Earth's horizon as an astronaut sees it from the Cupola, with the light moving across the planet through your day.

The same view from orbit over the eight planets and Pluto. Each world has its own sky: a smaller, fainter Sun the farther out you go, its own moon crossing every hour, Saturn's rings overhead, and a night side showing what spacecraft have actually seen there.

The edge shows how long light takes to travel from Earth to that world right now, worked out on your watch.

Right now, from Earth's orbit

Worked out in this page with the same maths the watch uses: from the date and time only, with no internet connection and no location.

Distance to the Sun comes from Earth's orbit: about 147.1 million km in early January and 152.1 million km in early July. Sunlight age is that distance divided by the speed of light. Travelled today is Earth's orbital speed, 29.3 to 30.3 km/s, multiplied by the time since your midnight.

The light on the planet follows solar time for your time zone, so it is close to, but not exactly, your local sunrise and sunset. Distances use a simplified orbit model, accurate to well under 1%.

Privacy policy

Cupola watch faces collect no personal data.

  • No accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking.
  • The faces request no permissions and do not connect to the internet.
  • Health, weather and other readings shown in slots come from apps already on your watch. They are displayed on the watch only; Cupola does not store or send them anywhere.
  • This website sets no cookies and loads nothing from other sites.

If this ever changes, this policy will be updated first. Contact: cupola.studio@gmail.com. Last updated 12 November 2026.
